## What the research supports

[W3C: Headings and Labels](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/headings-and-labels.html) supports descriptive organization and labels that explain purpose. It does not require removing a personal voice. That is why navigation/section orientation should remain recognizable while the display titles can be playful.

[NN/g: Layer-Cake Scanning](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/layer-cake-pattern-scanning/) describes readers using headings to find relevant passages. The practical inference here is consistent hierarchy and spacing: modest orientation labels, stronger meaningful section titles, and comfortable body copy. It does not establish an ideal font size for Greetings, fellow person! or predict signup rates for this site.

[Tuch et al.: Visual complexity and first impressions](https://research.google/pubs/the-role-of-visual-complexity-and-prototypicality-regarding-first-impression-of-websites-working-towards-understanding-aesthetic-judgments/) studies aesthetic judgments under experimental viewing conditions. It gives a reason to keep a quiet focal area and control visual complexity; it does not prove that a particular color, animation or graphic will make visitors subscribe. Zack’s feedback is the stronger evidence for reducing the distracting ambient light in this preview.

The practical marketing goal is a clear, memorable identity, a credible authored invitation and enjoyable exploration. Evidence does not justify claims that “warm colors guarantee trust” or that more motion guarantees engagement. The next evaluation is actual first-view readability, finding the main actions, and whether the quieter effects let Zack’s words and pictures lead.
